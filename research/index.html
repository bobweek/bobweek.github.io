Research

Evolutionary consequences of interspecific interactions

My research aims to understand how interactions among biological populations shape their evolution. I develop mathematical and computational approaches that connect individual-level biological processes to population, community, and evolutionary dynamics.

Much of my work involves extending foundational evolutionary theory to account for increasingly complex ecological systems, from single populations to coevolving species and host-associated microbial communities.

Foundations of stochastic evolutionary theory

How do individual-level stochastic processes give rise to population-level evolutionary dynamics?

I use stochastic processes to develop mathematical descriptions of biological populations undergoing ecological and evolutionary change. A particular interest is connecting individual-based processes to tractable descriptions of the dynamics of population abundance, phenotypic distributions, and genetic variation.

Current interests include:

  • Stochastic eco-evolutionary dynamics of quantitative traits
  • The evolution of genetic variance and covariance
  • Interactions between selection, genetic drift, and demography
  • Mathematical connections between individual-based models and population-level processes

Selected work:

  • Stochastic Eco-Evolutionary Dynamics of Multivariate Traits
  • A White Noise Approach to Evolutionary Ecology

Coevolution and interspecific interactions

How does evolution change when the selective environment is itself composed of evolving populations?

Interspecific interactions introduce ecological and evolutionary feedbacks that can fundamentally alter the trajectories of interacting populations. My research investigates the consequences of these feedbacks for mutualism, antagonism, local adaptation, and community persistence.

I am also interested in connecting coevolutionary theory to empirical observations and developing statistical approaches for uncovering the signatures of coevolution in nature.

Current interests include:

  • Coevolutionary arms races and the maintenance of mutualism
  • Geographic variation in coevolution and local adaptation
  • Evolutionary dynamics in spatially structured communities
  • Statistical inference of coevolutionary processes
  • The relationship between mechanisms of interaction and evolutionary outcomes

Selected work:

  • Host-Parasite Coevolution in Continuous Space
  • Coevolutionary Arms Races and the Conditions for the Maintenance of Mutualism
  • The Measurement of Coevolution in the Wild
  • Uncovering Cryptic Coevolution

Host-microbiome ecology and evolution

How does evolutionary theory change when an organism’s phenotype is partly determined by an ecological community?

Host-associated microbial communities introduce additional sources of phenotypic variation and inheritance. Unlike host genes, microbes can be acquired, lost, and transmitted among individuals throughout their lifetimes. Understanding the evolutionary consequences of these processes requires integrating population genetics, quantitative genetics, community ecology, and epidemiology.

My research develops theoretical foundations for understanding the evolution of microbiome-mediated traits and the reciprocal relationships between host evolution and microbial community dynamics.

Current interests include:

  • Quantitative genetics of microbiome-mediated traits
  • Microbial transmission as a mechanism of inheritance
  • Microbiome-mediated adaptation and evolutionary rescue
  • Microbial community assembly within and among hosts
  • Social and spatial networks as determinants of microbiome diversity and evolution

Selected work:

  • Quantitative Genetics of Microbiome Mediated Traits
  • Applying Evolutionary Theory to Understand Host-Microbiome Evolution
  • Microbially Assisted Rescue of Host Populations
