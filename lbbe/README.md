# Seminar: figures and deck

"Charting the evolutionary consequences of interspecific interactions: from
theory to inference." Fifty-five figures, the scripts that make them, and a
Quarto reveal.js deck that puts them in order.

```bash
python3 scripts/make_all.py                    # all figures -> figures/
python3 scripts/make_contact_sheet.py          # figures/_contact_sheet*.png, in talk order
python3 scripts/make_deck.py                   # seminar.qmd from the slide table
quarto render seminar.qmd                      # seminar.html + seminar_files/

SEMINAR_MONO=1      python3 scripts/make_all.py   # pure greyscale
SEMINAR_STRICT=1    python3 scripts/make_all.py   # fail on any text collision
SEMINAR_RECOMPUTE=1 python3 scripts/make_all.py   # re-run the cached simulations
```

Needs numpy, scipy, matplotlib (>= 3.8), Pillow, and the Inter font (falls
back to TeX Gyre Heros / Liberation Sans). The chart uses TeX Gyre Pagella
(falls back to any Palatino). Figures that place a published image read it
from `assets/` and draw without it if the file is absent. Three simulations
are slow enough to cache; their results are in `data/*.npz` and are re-used
unless `SEMINAR_RECOMPUTE=1` (about three minutes in all).

Look first at `figures/_contact_sheet.png` (talk order) and
`figures/_contact_sheet_grey.png`.

The bundle comes as two zips that unpack into the same `seminar_deck/` folder.
`seminar_deck_v12.zip` is everything needed to show, edit and rebuild the
deck: scripts, data, assets, the figures as PNG, and the rendered deck.
`seminar_deck_v12_extras.zip` adds the vector PDFs of the figures, the
greyscale proofs and the two contact sheets; all of it is regenerated by
`make_all.py` and `make_contact_sheet.py`.

## The deck

`seminar.qmd` is generated by `scripts/make_deck.py` from one table: title,
part, eyebrow, status badge, figure, speaker notes (with the cumulative times
of the 47-minute plan). Edit the table, not the qmd. `deck.css` is the
stylesheet of the research-vignettes deck, unchanged; `seminar.css` overrides
it so that a slide is a one-line title, a small eyebrow and the figure at its
native 1180 x 560. A title that wraps to two lines shrinks the figure rather
than overflowing. `seminar_standalone.html` is the same deck with everything
embedded in one file.

`seminar.html` needs the folder `seminar_files/` beside it (and `figures/`,
`assets/`, the two stylesheets); keep them together. If both versions are
rendered, render the standalone first: rendering it second removes
`seminar_files/`.

```bash
quarto render seminar.qmd -M embed-resources:true -o seminar_standalone.html
quarto render seminar.qmd
```

From v12 the rendered deck has been opened in a headless browser (Chromium,
1280 x 720): all 54 sections load, no image is missing, and nothing falls
outside the slide. The deck's web fonts come from Google Fonts, so the first
time it is shown on a new machine it needs a connection, or Space Grotesk,
Inter and JetBrains Mono installed locally. Still step through it once on the
machine and projector you will use.

Multi-frame sequences are consecutive slides (9a, 9b; 19a, 19b, 19c; 22a,
22b). Slides 9a to 11b share one camera: every node sits at the same place on
each, so with the deck's fade transition the picture is built up and redrawn
rather than replaced. Ten backup slides follow the close.

The title slide's chart is whitened just under the words by a CSS ellipse
(`section.withmap::after` in `seminar.css`); its two pairs of percentages set
the size and centre. There is no
text-only slide: the reference list is in `REFERENCES.md`.

## Design contract (`scripts/semstyle.py`)

* Canvases are in slide pixels (deck 1280 x 720; `FULL` = 1180 x 560). Type
  sizes are literal: labels 20 px, annotations 17 px, nothing below 15 px.
  PNGs at 2x, plus a PDF.
* Colour is additive, never the only channel (shape, dash, hatch carry it
  too). A greyscale proof is written on every build.
* A collision check runs on every build.
* Every figure has a status line: ● published, ◐ in prep., ○ planned,
  □ schematic. Nothing unpublished is drawn as a finding.

## Ports and re-computations

**`scripts/qgmmt_sim.py`** (slides 24b, 24c). The simulation of Week et al.
(2025), ported from `fcts_strcts.jl`, `figs34.jl`, `fig5.jl`. It reproduces
the published Fig. 3 (end points about 20, 58, 125 after ten generations) and
the pattern of Fig. 5, and the build asserts both orderings. Differences from
the Julia code: vectorised over hosts; additive variances taken as the
variance of the additive value (the Julia regression returns the same number,
because the value is an exact linear function of the predictors); fewer
replicates for Fig. 5 (60 parameter draws x 36, against 100 x 400). The
selection strength is the repository's s = 0.1, which matches the scale of the
published figure; the paper's text quotes 10^-3.

**`scripts/abc_sim.py`** (slide 20). A small vectorised port of
`ABC_Coevo_3_1_19.cpp`: the same life cycle (selection by numerical
integration of the logistic arms-race fitness, drift, island-model movement,
mating), the same five summary statistics, flat priors on the two strengths of
coevolutionary selection, and the source's acceptance thresholds.
Simplifications: a fixed 200 generations instead of the equilibrium test, a
30-point integration grid, and background parameters fixed at the modes of the
source's priors. The posteriors on the slide come from data simulated with
known strengths (0.6 and 1.4, marked); 194 of 6,000 draws are kept. It is an
illustration of the method, not the weevil–camellia result.

**`scripts/coev_likelihood.py`** (slide 19). `functions.R` transliterated line
for line. The build asserts that the fitted model reproduces the sample
moments exactly and, for the fly and flower, the paper's Table 2: B = 6.40e-5
and 1.84e-6, p2 = 1.19e-7 (p1 is below machine precision in the paper;
2e-35 here), C = 1.08e-5, balance 0.184, and trait increases of 134 % and
34.5 %. Intervals use a different random stream from the paper's.

**`scripts/qme_rescue_model.py`** (slides 25–27, 29). The manuscript's model
code, unchanged. Slide 26 uses the rescue scenarios (β = 1, ℓ = 0.4); slides
27 and 29 use the statement's manifold parameters (β = 10, ℓ = 4, s_g = 0.03,
s_m = 0.05, e = 0.10).

## Notes on individual figures

**`s03_mapB`, `s31_closing_map`** Each system sits on a soft white plate
(three nested translucent layers, about three-quarters opaque at the centre;
`_veil(strength=...)` in `s03_map.py`) so the glyphs and words stand out and
the ring and spokes recede. "Predator and prey" is the second example of a
same-scale pair; "host and parasite" sits with host and microbe. The eight lesson lines on `s31` are draft
wording.

**`s06_interface`** Pauw et al.'s own panels, cropped and rearranged; only the
headings and the two lines under panel C are added. The source image is 680
px wide, so the panels are soft.

**`s04_approach`, `s18_backward`** The population panel is
`scripts/toy_ibm.py`: a toy individual-based simulation of a predator and its
prey in a habitat patch (about ten thousand individuals; local interactions on
a coarse grid; predation succeeds when the two traits are alike, so prey
evolve away and predators follow). It is not a model from any of the papers
and not a result; it is dense on purpose, to suggest the many-individual
limit. Four snapshots: the start, the first prey peak, the first predator
peak, the end. Hue is species, darkness is trait. The strips are the two
abundances and the two trait means ± s.d. from the same run (cached in
`data/toy_ibm_run.npz`). The formula in "analytical approximation" is a
generic example, written as given. The ellipses are from `s05_drift_G`
(Week 2026). The scatter and histogram under "measurable patterns" are
placeholders. Both arrows back end at the process panel. Slide 18 is the same
drawing with those arrows and that panel emphasised.

**`s09`–`s11`** Named plants and their order by tube length: Goldblatt &
Manning (2000), Table 2. "About 35 plant species" is a scripted count of that
table's converted text; check it against the PDF. Each pollinator is drawn
above the centre of its own visiting range, so the same positions serve the
network, the projection and the resource axis.

**`s11c_circle`** α, β, γ for all 36 pairs from Week et al. (2021), Eq. 35
(SM.109–110), with each species' position and curve width from slide 11b and
equal abundances, niche use and competition strength. Grey curves: the same
formulas with one niche width for everyone (the guild's median), on which
every pair would then sit; the scatter around them comes from the unequal
widths. In this guild corr(α, β) = 0 exactly, corr(α, |β|) = 0.93,
corr(α, γ) = 0.05. Against the paper: the first two agree with its statement
(no relation with direction; positive relation with magnitude). The third is
weaker than its statement that quadratic gradients also tend to correlate
positively, and the curve shows why: γ is positive only for nearly coincident
niches, which this evenly spaced nine-species guild lacks. Not on the slide:
evaluating the rich-community integrals of SM 6.3 at this guild's niche width
and trait variance gives about 0.72 and 0.68, both positive; the second looks
higher than the dark region of the paper's Fig. 2, which is worth a check by
someone who knows how that heatmap was computed.

**`s14_mosaic`** Only the idea is taken from the geographic-mosaic papers.

**`s16_model`** The surfaces are illustrations with the model's covariance
form, not a model run (deliberately: the model is expensive).

**`s17_scale`, `s17x_grid`** The paper's Fig. 3, recomputed from Eq. 7b with the
cross-covariance obtained in closed form from the supplement's cross-spectrum
(Eq. S11c; `cross_cov` in `s14_17_space.py`, checked against numerical
integration). Signs and shapes match the published panels; the vertical scale
differs from the published one by a constant factor of about 1.6 that I could
not recover from the text, so the axis marks zero only. Right: the
repository's computed values for Fig. 4, unchanged. Those two parameter sets
have biotic selection as strong as abiotic selection, so the weak-selection
formula does not reproduce them and they are not recomputed.

**v7.**

*Approach slides.* `toy_ibm.py` again: individuals live on a plane (wrap-around
edges) whose habitat quality is a sum of long waves with drifting phases, so
sparse areas and the edges of dense ones move through time. Predation uses a
trait-matching kernel averaged over the other species' trait distribution;
offspring traits are the average of parent and a random mate plus noise; weak
stabilising selection holds both traits near zero. The result is Red Queen
cycles in the two trait means (period about 65 time units) with the abundances
cycling alongside. Still a toy, not a model from the papers.
