## Foundations of stochastic evolutionary theory

How do individual-level stochastic processes give rise to
population-level evolutionary dynamics?

I use stochastic processes to develop mathematical descriptions
of biological populations undergoing ecological and evolutionary
change. A particular interest is connecting individual-based
processes to tractable descriptions of the dynamics of population
abundance, phenotypic distributions, and genetic variation.

Current interests include:

- Stochastic eco-evolutionary dynamics of quantitative traits
- The evolution of genetic variance and covariance
- Interactions between selection, genetic drift, and demography
- Mathematical connections between individual-based models and

## Coevolution and interspecific interactions

How does evolution change when the selective environment is itself
composed of evolving populations?

Interspecific interactions introduce ecological and evolutionary
feedbacks that can fundamentally alter the trajectories of
interacting populations. My research investigates the consequences
of these feedbacks for mutualism, antagonism, local adaptation,
and community persistence.

I am also interested in connecting coevolutionary theory to
empirical observations and developing statistical approaches
for uncovering the signatures of coevolution in nature.

Current interests include:

- Coevolutionary arms races and the maintenance of mutualism
- Geographic variation in coevolution and local adaptation
- Evolutionary dynamics in spatially structured communities
- Statistical inference of coevolutionary processes
- The relationship between mechanisms of interaction and
  evolutionary outcomes

**Selected work:**

- [Host-Parasite Coevolution in Continuous Space](https://doi.org/10.1086/727470)

## Host-microbiome ecology and evolution

How does evolutionary theory change when an organism's phenotype
is partly determined by an ecological community?

Host-associated microbial communities introduce additional
sources of phenotypic variation and inheritance. Unlike host
genes, microbes can be acquired, lost, and transmitted among
individuals throughout their lifetimes. Understanding the
evolutionary consequences of these processes requires integrating
population genetics, quantitative genetics, community ecology,
and epidemiology.

My research develops theoretical foundations for understanding
the evolution of microbiome-mediated traits and the reciprocal
relationships between host evolution and microbial community
dynamics.

Current interests include:

- Quantitative genetics of microbiome-mediated traits
- Microbial transmission as a mechanism of inheritance
- Microbiome-mediated adaptation and evolutionary rescue
- Microbial community assembly within and among hosts
- Social and spatial networks as determinants of microbiome
  diversity and evolution
